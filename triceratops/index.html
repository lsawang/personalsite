Case Study 01

Turning a club’s scattered Discord rules into one scannable web page

Triceratops: UX research and Rules page design for a UC Davis club’s first website.

Triceratops is a UC Davis club that crochets dinosaurs and hides them across campus for students to find. It ran entirely on Discord and Instagram, so newcomers had no clear way in. My team of 6 designed and built the club’s first public website, and I owned the Rules page from research through code.

What this meant for the Rules page

It had to answer three questions fast: what’s allowed, how many dinos I can claim, and what to do once I find one.

Design decisions:

Skim first, depth underneath

Taking a step back

My first hi-fi leaned heavily on animation. Testers said the pop-ups and motion got in the way, so I cut motion back to hover states on clickable items only, which gave the page a calmer, more premium feel. See the before and after

Design system:

A design system built from members’ own words

Design system by Nathan Salas

In interviews, members described the club as colorful, whimsical, “like a crayon box.” That became the brief: warm and inviting, with rounded, soft shapes and no sharp edges. I matched the Rules page to this system once it was set.

Final design:

One page, built to be scanned on any screen

Conduct rules, hunt rules, flash sales, post-claim steps, then an FAQ that hands off to #rules-help, in the order a new hunter needs them.

Impact:

The site launched at the UC Davis Involvement Fair.

The site went live on Oct 13, 2026. The fair drove most of that week’s attention, so I split the numbers into what the site did and what the launch did around it.
