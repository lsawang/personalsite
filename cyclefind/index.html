Case Study 02

Designing a private, stigma-free app to find free period products nearby

CycleFind: a menstrual equity app that pairs a resource map with cycle tracking.

A menstrual equity app that connects people with nearby free and low-cost products, and gives them a reason to come back. This was an individual project, so I owned it end to end, from research through two rounds of usability testing.

Problem:

Period products are basic necessities. Access to them isn’t.

Period poverty affects people who can’t consistently afford menstrual products, and cost isn’t the only barrier. Even when campuses, shelters, and health centers offer free products, people still have to find them, check they’re in stock, and figure out how to get them.

Research also pointed to stigma. Someone looking for a pad shouldn’t have to announce they need financial help, create an account, or arrive to find the shelf empty.

How might I make finding menstrual resources feel as private, reliable, and ordinary as looking up any other place on a map?

Research:

The resource isn’t useful if people don’t trust it.

My primary audience became people roughly 14–26, mostly high school and college students with limited income, including people with limited mobile data or unstable housing.

The principle that came out of it

Make asking for help feel like finding information.

Design decisions:

Finding products should feel like finding a coffee shop.

Flow 01: Finding a resource

The primary flow gets someone from opening the app to a usable resource with as few steps as possible.

Little friction

Flow 02: Cycle tracking

Cycle tracking builds a bridge between planning and accessing products, so the app is useful before an emergency.

All in one place

Design system:

Health-related didn’t have to mean clinical.

Instead of a sparse clinical palette, I leaned into red burgundy, maroon, and dusty rose for a warm, recognizable identity. Creme and toast backgrounds keep resource information readable and easy on the eyes.
