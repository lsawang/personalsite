CycleFind

Finding menstrual products shouldn't be another source of stress.

Designing a menstrual equity app that connects people with nearby free and low-cost products, while giving them a reason to come back.

The Problem

Period products are basic necessities. Access to them isn't always basic.

Period poverty affects people who can't consistently afford menstrual products, but the problem isn't limited to cost. Even when free products are available through campuses, shelters, health centers, and community organizations, people still have to figure out where they are, whether they’re actually in stock, and how to access them.

My research pointed to another issue that was harder to design around: stigma. Someone looking for a pad shouldn't have to announce that they need financial help, create an account, or arrive somewhere only to discover that the products are gone or not there.

How might I make finding menstrual resources feel as private, reliable, and ordinary as looking up any other place on a map?

Research

The resource isn't useful if people don't trust it.

My primary audience became younger users roughly 14-26, mostly high school and college students with limited income, this includes people with limited mobile data and/or housing insecurity.

Participants consistently described the app as straightforward, and the issues were small moments of friction rather than navigation failures. In the second round the Log Today action was easier to find, onboarding felt more natural, spacing felt less cramped, and the language was clearer. All the features were praised and most said they would actually use the app.
